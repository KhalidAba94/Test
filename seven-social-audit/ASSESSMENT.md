## 2. What the content looks like

**Content pillars (Aug–Nov 2026):**
1. **Destination promotion:** SEVEN Tabuk and SEVEN Abha (go-karting, Hot Wheels e-Karting, Cyber Bowling, GOLFI mini-golf, arcades, dining). This is the bulk of output.
2. **Activation and booking CTAs:** “احجزوا من الرابط في البايو”, school-holiday plans, group bookings, the app download push (Jul 2026).
3. **People and employer brand:** “Behind the Joy / A day with Noorah Alotaibi”, and “engineers of joy” (Ziad & co.). This is a revival of the 2023 “مهندس البهجة” series.
4. **National moments:** Saudi National Day 96 (#عزنا_بطبعنا), and “إنجاز بدأ بوعد”.
5. **Trend and meme content (TikTok only):** a dancing-character loop, #gta6 bait.

**Language:** Arabic-first. 9/10 TikToks and 10/16 Snaps are Arabic-only, and the rest add English lines. That fits a Saudi family/youth audience. English is used inconsistently rather than as a deliberate strategy.

**Format:** Vertical short video (6–45 s) dominates TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube Shorts. YouTube long-form is mostly 0:45–1:40 corporate/destination films.

## 3. Performance

### TikTok: the healthiest organic channel
- Latest 10 videos (7–19 Nov): **median 1,762 views**, mean 3,896, total ~39K.
- **Engagement rate (likes+comments+shares ÷ views): median 3.4%**, range 1.0–4.6%. That's solid for a brand account.
- **Breakout:** the “كلووو يرقص” dancing-character loop drew **25.1K views (≈14× median)** and the most shares (53). Its 1.0% ER shows it travelled beyond followers. Trend-native, character-led humour outperforms straight venue promos.
- Questions and tagging prompts (“من في قروبكم مدير الطلبات…؟ منشنوهم”, “وش الشعور…؟”) earned the highest comment counts (6–15). Conversation prompts work.
- **Weak spot:** Posting is very new or bursty. All 10 visible videos fall within 13 days, with 3 on 19 Nov alone. 94.8K lifetime likes on 8.9K followers suggests a thin back-catalogue.

### YouTube: paid reach, organic silence
- 8.06M lifetime views, but **six videos account for 99%**: 5.8M (2023 launch film), 832K (Joy in Tabuk), 540K (National Day 94), 251K (Jazan), 242K (Doors of Joy in Abha), 58K (Hot Wheels).
- Those big videos have almost no likes: **832K views → 76 likes (0.009%)**, 242K → 36, 58K → 4. That's the signature of **in-stream/discovery ad spend**. The views are bought impressions that don't convert to subscribers (1.62K).
- Organic uploads in 2026 have a **median of 295 views**. Likes on those are healthy as a share of views (2–3%), but the absolute numbers are tiny.
- **Cadence gap:** 21 uploads in 2023 → 4 in 2024 → 2 in 2025 → 15 in 2026 (since Aug). The channel went dormant for almost 2 years.
- Titles are Arabic-only or mixed. Descriptions are thin, there are no chapters, and playlists per destination appear unused.

### Snapchat: consistent and on-strategy, but unmeasurable
- 16 Spotlights in 6 weeks (**≈2.7/week**), with steady weekly cadence and Tabuk/Abha focus. This is the right platform for KSA under-30s.
- Logged-out Spotlight shows no view counts, and comments are empty (“Be the first to comment”). Performance has to come from Snapchat Insights.

### Instagram: the biggest audience, no visible strategy evidence
- 84.1K followers is SEVEN’s largest owned audience, about 9× TikTok and 50× YouTube. We couldn't inspect posts logged-out. **This is the #1 gap in this audit.** Pull Meta Business Suite data before drawing conclusions.

## 4. Strengths
- Clear, ownable brand idea: **“البهجة / Joy”** with “عوالمها بهجة / Multiverse of Joy”, used consistently across platforms.
- Strong IP partnerships to leverage (Hot Wheels, Warner Bros. Discovery, Clip ’n Climb, Transformers announced).
- Short-form cross-posting is already happening (the same bowling spot ran on TikTok, Snapchat and YouTube Shorts on 18 Nov), so production can be reused.
- People-led storytelling (employees, “engineers of joy”) is on-trend and builds employer brand.
- Arabic-first, Saudi-dialect copy feels native rather than translated.

## 5. Weaknesses and risks
1. **Identity drift:** PIF vs Qiddiya ownership, “Saudi Entertainment Ventures” vs “SEVEN” vs “سڤن”, and two Facebook URLs (EntertainKSA and SaudiSEVEN). The YouTube channel’s LinkedIn link points to an *admin* URL (`/company/13740813/admin/`), which is broken for the public.
2. **Hashtag hygiene:** variants split reach: #سڤن_أبها vs #سڤن_ابها, #سڤن_تبوك‏ with hidden RTL marks, and tags glued to punctuation (#SEVEN_Tabuk, / #سڤن_تبوك!). There's no single campaign tag per destination.
3. **YouTube is a paid-media dumping ground,** not a channel. Big ad-view numbers flatter dashboards but build no subscriber base, and the 2-year gap signals neglect.
4. **Captions are CTA-heavy and venue-generic.** Most say “come have fun / book now” with little reason-to-visit (price, new attraction, event date, offer).
5. **Destinations are under-represented:** content is almost entirely Tabuk and Abha. Riyadh (Al Hamra), Madinah, Yanbu and Jazan feature only in older announcement films, with no progress updates since.
6. **Engagement is one-way:** questions are posted, but there's no visible community replies or UGC reposts.

## 7. Gaps and how to close them
This audit is limited to what each platform shows a logged-out visitor:
- **Instagram, Facebook, X, LinkedIn:** no posts captured. Closing the gap needs a logged-in browser session (Claude in Chrome on a desktop) or exports from Meta Business Suite, X Analytics and LinkedIn Page Analytics.
- **TikTok:** only the latest 10 videos are exposed by the public embed.
- **YouTube:** exact dates and likes are known only for the 15 most recent uploads (from the public RSS feed). Older dates are approximate (“3 years ago”), and comment counts weren't captured because YouTube rate-limited per-video pages.
- **Snapchat:** no view counts are public.

## 8. Update, 20 Nov 2026: combined with the weekly tracker and the 6 Nov analysis

This section adds three sources: the SEVEN / Scene weekly social tracker (27 account rows, 24 Sep – 19 Nov, follower counts re-verified on native profiles on 20 Nov), the 6 Nov social media analysis, and the 20 Nov evidence archive. The full presentation built from all four sources is the Slides deck (18 slides).

**What the tracker changes in this assessment**

- **TikTok is the growth story.** Followers went from 4,197 (24 Sep) to 8,937 (19 Nov), up 113%. The gain was +2,377 in the two weeks to 12 Nov and +1,096 in the week to 19 Nov. The 10K-by-31-Dec target now needs about 180 a week, so it should be reached in early December. The rise coincides with visible video posting (10 videos, 7–19 Nov) and the 25.1K-view breakout.
- **Instagram is compounding.** It went from 79.8K (1 Oct) to 84.3K (19 Nov). Weekly gains rose from +300 to +900, and the last two weeks averaged about +800. That is the largest absolute gain of any tracked account.
- **X is flat on followers** (46.5K → 46.7K), as it is for every tracked account. A typical post reaches 3.0–3.3% of followers, above the 3% target and above every cinema and mall. The 17 Nov “One destination. Endless possibilities.” post drew 16K views and 176 interactions (11 per 1,000), the best ratio captured on a large SEVEN post.
- **Booking path: the first progress.** Between 13 Oct and 5 Nov, 0 of 24 captured posts carried a link. Now 3 of the 5 newest X posts (12–19 Nov) carry a seven.sa link or card. TikTok still says “link in bio”, Instagram’s bio led with the App Store, and nothing is tracked per post.
- **Paid views on X too.** The 4 Oct X post had 735.3K views and 41 interactions (0.06 per 1,000), the same pattern as the YouTube ad films (831.8K views, 76 likes). X Ads data, agreed on 16 Oct, still hasn't been shared, and the tracker has no paid flag.
- **Abha is returning to the calendar.** 5 of 16 X and Instagram creatives named Abha between 13 Oct and 5 Nov, and none did in the week to 5 Nov. 4 of 10 TikToks named Abha between 7 and 19 Nov, but only 2 of 16 Snaps did.
- **Peers.** Six Flags QC has a similar Instagram audience (80.3K) but nearly 8× SEVEN’s TikTok (68.6K) and 8.7% X reach per post: that shows SEVEN's headroom. Scene is flat everywhere: X reach fell from 3.0% to 1.9%, and 0 of its 13 posts linked to booking, against VOX's 8 of 8.

**Revised priorities:**
1. Tracked booking links on every booking post, on every platform.
2. Settle paid spend: get the ads data and add a paid flag.
3. Scale the TikTok formats that worked.
4. Run coded offers per site.
5. Post at least 2 Abha creatives a week.
6. Fix identity and hashtag hygiene.
7. Revive YouTube Shorts.
8. Link Scene's film posts to booking.

Proposed targets and the open data asks are on the last three slides of the deck.
