Energy · drivers and watch-points

2026 profit came from price during a supply disruption

Aramco, 9M 2026 vs 9M 2025

  • Realised crude price +29.2% ($91.7 vs $71.0 a barrel); liquids output −15.2%.
  • On crude revenue, price added about SAR 155bn and lower volume took away about SAR 114bn.
  • Profit +36.1%, or +22.0% on Aramco’s own adjusted basis.
  • FY2022–25: capex rose 35% while free cash flow fell from SAR 557bn to 320bn.

The other five companies

  • Combined losses in FY2023–FY2025 turned into SAR 17.1bn of profit in 9M 2026.
  • Bahri: revenue SAR 26.1bn vs 7.1bn on crisis tanker rates; profit SAR 10.0bn.
  • Petro Rabigh: from a SAR 3.3bn loss to SAR 6.7bn profit on crisis refining margins.
  • Drillers weaker: Arabian Drilling revenue −8.1%; ADES −18.3% excluding acquisitions.

So what

2027 revenue rises only if Saudi output recovers well above its 2026 average; Bahri and Petro Rabigh will give back crisis gains if flows normalise.

Source: company filings; Aramco 9M 2026 interim report (prices, production)

Detail: A8 bridges · A9 without Aramco

The 2026 rebound is price, not volume: Aramco sold less oil at much higher prices during the Hormuz disruption. Its reported profit also includes a SAR 25.9bn one-off gain, which is why its adjusted growth is lower. Outside Aramco the swing is larger in percentage terms: shipping and refining had record periods, while the drillers weakened as offshore work was suspended. ADES reported growth of 44% only because of acquisitions; excluding them its revenue fell 18%.
