Banks · drivers and watch-points

Growth came from lending volume; funding is now the constraint

What drove FY2022–FY2025

  • Loans grew 12.3% a year and deposits 8.7%, so loans rose from 97% to 107% of deposits (30 Sep 2026).
  • The margin on assets narrowed from 2.91% to 2.74% as deposit costs rose with rates.
  • Costs grew 5.9% a year against income at 10.0%: cost-to-income fell 3.5 points.
  • Cost of risk fell from 46 bp to 30 bp, lifting profit above income growth.

What to watch

  • Two banks dominate: Al Rajhi and SNB earn 49% of sector income and 54% of profit.
  • 9M 2026 impairments rose 18.3%; Al Rajhi accounts for about two-thirds of the rise.
  • Funding gaps: loans are 117% of deposits at Riyad and 113% at BSF and SNB.
  • Tier 1 sukuk rose from SAR 54bn (end-2022) to SAR 146bn (30 Sep 2026).

So what

2027 earnings depend more on funding costs and credit quality than on loan demand.

Detail: A4 growth by bank · A5 margins and funding

Volume, not price: lending grew faster than deposits and the margin on assets fell. Profit still beat income because of cost discipline and low credit costs, which may not repeat. Concentration matters: Al Rajhi and SNB together are about half the sector. The 9M 2026 impairment rise is concentrated at Al Rajhi; SNB's 9M charge was almost nil after recoveries. Loans-to-deposits here is the simple ratio; SAMA's regulatory ratio uses a different definition and is below the 90% cap at all ten banks.
